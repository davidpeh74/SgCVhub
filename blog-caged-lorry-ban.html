Caged Lorry Ban from 1 Jan 2027: What Singapore Businesses Need to Do

Regulations · 4 min read · 16 Nov 2026

From 1 January 2027, companies in Singapore will no longer be allowed to ferry workers in the caged deck of a lorry. The Ministry of Transport (MOT) and the Land Transport Authority (LTA) announced the ban in March 2026 as part of efforts to improve worker transport safety. If your crew rides in the back of a lorry, here is what changes and what you can do about it.

What is changing

The ban covers ferrying workers in caged lorry decks. The reason given is safety: in an emergency, a cage gate that is latched or locked from the outside could trap the workers inside.

Companies that do not comply from 1 January 2027 will face penalties. MOT and LTA said more details would be shared before the ban takes effect, so check LTA's website for the latest before the deadline.

What is not changing

Caged lorries can still be used to carry goods and equipment. The ban is about carrying people, not about the lorry itself.

Your options if you carry workers

1. Convert the rear deck

Companies that want to keep ferrying workers or equipment can convert the rear deck by adding a canopy and side railings. Check with LTA whether the modification needs approval or inspection before you start, and allow time for the work to be done before 1 January.

2. Replace the lorry

If your current lorry is old, or the cost and downtime of a conversion add up, replacing it can make more sense. This is also a good moment to look at trade-in value on the old unit, or scrapping it if it is at the end of its life.

3. Until the ban takes effect

Until 1 January 2027, companies must make sure workers have safe access to and from the lorry deck.

A quick checklist

  1. Do any of your lorries carry workers in a caged deck?
  2. If yes, decide whether to convert the deck or replace the lorry.
  3. Confirm with LTA whether a conversion needs approval, and how long it will take.
  4. Plan the work well before 1 January 2027, not in the last week of December.
  5. Keep an eye out for LTA's penalty details, due before the ban starts.
